# Debt Cycles (Dalio framework)

## The core mechanic
Credit creates spending power that is not backed by current production. Spending
financed by credit is someone else's income, so credit expansion is
self-reinforcing on the way up and self-reinforcing on the way down. Debt is a
promise to deliver future money; a debt crisis is the discovery that the promises
in aggregate exceed what can be delivered.

## Two cycles running at once
- **Short-term (business) cycle, ~5-8 years.** Central bank tightens to contain
  inflation, demand falls, slack builds, then it eases. Resolved with interest
  rates.
- **Long-term (debt supercycle), ~50-75 years.** Each short-term cycle ends with
  more debt than it began with, because policymakers ease more than they tighten.
  Eventually debt service consumes so much income that rates cannot go low enough
  to sustain it. This is *not* resolvable with interest rates alone.

## The endgame of the long cycle: four levers
When debt burdens become unsustainable, policymakers use some mix of:
1. **Austerity** — deflationary, politically hardest, rarely sufficient alone.
2. **Debt default / restructuring** — deflationary, concentrated pain on creditors.
3. **Wealth transfers** — taxing the haves, redistributive, politically contentious.
4. **Debt monetisation / currency debasement** — inflationary, diffuse, least
   visible to the electorate, and therefore the historically preferred path.

**The critical asymmetry: levers 1 and 2 are deflationary, 4 is inflationary.**
A "beautiful deleveraging" balances them so nominal growth exceeds nominal rates
without runaway inflation. Which lever dominates determines whether the regime is
deflationary (bonds win, cash is king) or inflationary (hard assets win, cash is
trash). **Reading which lever is being pulled is the single most important
regime question for gold, silver, and BTC.**

## Why this matters for hard assets
Gold is a bet against the *purchasing power of the currency*, not primarily a bet
on fear. Its bull markets coincide with periods when real rates are negative or
falling and when confidence in the currency's future purchasing power erodes.
Silver is the same trade with an industrial-demand overlay and roughly 2-3x the
volatility. Bitcoin is a newer, higher-beta expression of the same debasement
thesis, but with a liquidity/risk-appetite component that can dominate over
short horizons — which is why BTC sometimes trades like a tech stock and
sometimes like digital gold, and knowing which regime is active matters.

## Late-cycle markers to watch for
- Debt service consuming a rising share of government revenue
- Central bank buying an increasing share of new government issuance
- Yield curve control, buybacks, or other duration-management operations
- Financial repression (see the debasement note)
- Reserve managers diversifying out of the reserve currency into gold
- The currency weakening *while* long yields rise — the classic loss-of-confidence
  signature, and distinct from ordinary rate-driven currency strength

## Reserve-currency lifecycle
Reserve status is earned through trade competitiveness and military/financial
reach, exploited through the ability to borrow cheaply in one's own currency, and
lost through over-borrowing and the printing that follows. The decline is slow,
then sudden: the currency remains dominant in usage long after it stops being
trusted as a store of value. Watch the divergence between *transactional* use
(still high) and *reserve* use (falling) — that gap is where gold accumulation by
central banks shows up.
